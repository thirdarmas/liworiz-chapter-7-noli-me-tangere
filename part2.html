The First Novel

Noli Me Tangere

A story of Crisóstomo Ibarra, María Clara, Elias, and the society they lived in.

José Rizal · 1887
The Story

What is Noli Me Tangere about?

The novel presents the struggles and injustices experienced by Filipinos during Spanish colonial rule through the lives and conflicts of its characters.

Central figure

Crisóstomo Ibarra

A young Filipino who returns home after studying in Europe and hopes to contribute to his country by improving education.

Crisóstomo Ibarra returns to the Philippines after studying in Europe. He wants to help his country, particularly through education.

However, his plans are challenged by the realities of colonial society. He encounters discrimination, abusive friars, corruption, and other forms of injustice.

His story is connected with the lives of María Clara and Elias, whose own experiences reveal different sides of the society Rizal wanted his readers to see.

After the story: María Clara enters the Santa Clara convent despite Padre Damaso's pleading. Padre Damaso is found dead in his new parish, Capitan Tiago becomes an opium addict, and Doña Victorina keeps harassing her husband.

The novel does more than tell a love story. It exposes the conditions and injustices experienced by Filipinos under colonial rule.
01

Return

After seven years in Europe, Ibarra returns and is welcomed with a party by Capitan Tiago, María Clara's father. Padre Damaso shows coldness and rudeness, to the surprise of the guests.

02

The Truth About His Father

Lt. Guevarra tells Ibarra that Don Rafael died in prison after accidentally pushing a tax collector who was hitting a boy. His body was buried in a Chinese cemetery, then ordered removed and thrown into the river.

03

Sisa and Pilosopo Tasyo

Ibarra meets Pilosopo Tasyo, whose ideas were ahead of his time. The saddest story is Sisa's: a once-rich girl made poor by a gambler husband, who loses her sons Crispin and Basilio and goes insane.

04

The Picnic

Ibarra, María Clara, her friends Sinang, Neneng, Victoria, and Iday, and Capitan Tiago go on a picnic with the young boatman Elias. Elias nearly dies fighting a crocodile, and Ibarra jumps in to save him.

05

Plot Against Ibarra

Ibarra plans a school in San Diego. At the laying of the cornerstone, Elias warns him of a plot, and he is pushed away from a collapsing derrick just in time.

06

Conflict with Damaso

When Padre Damaso repeatedly insults his father's memory at a dinner, Ibarra loses control and attacks him with a knife. He is excommunicated and his engagement with María Clara is broken.

07

Elias's Story

Elias tells Ibarra about his family's tragedies. When Ibarra is falsely accused of an attack on the guardia civil barracks, Elias burns the evidence and helps him escape.

08

María Clara's Secret

María Clara tells Ibarra that Padre Salvi blackmailed her into giving up his letters to protect her mother's reputation. She also reveals that her real father is Padre Damaso.

09

Escape and Epilogue

Elias is shot while diverting the guardia civil from Ibarra's banca on the Pasig River. He dies in the forest, urging Basilio to bury him and his mother's body. The epilogue shows what became of the others.

The novel's social message becomes clearer through the different experiences of its characters.

Ibarra's beloved

María Clara

Ibarra's beloved, whose life is also affected by the social and religious structures surrounding her.

Voice of injustice

Elias

The Lost Chapter

Elias and Salome The chapter that did not appear in the printed novel.

“Elias and Salome” was part of Rizal's original manuscript and was intended as Chapter 25. Rizal removed it before the novel was printed, so it does not appear in the published version of Noli Me Tangere.

What was it about?

The chapter focuses on Elias and Salome, the woman he loves. It reveals a more personal side of Elias and shows his desire for a peaceful life despite the difficult circumstances surrounding him.

Why is it important?

The chapter allows readers to see Elias not only as someone connected to injustice, but also as a person who wants love, safety, and happiness.

Five moments
01

Elias and Salome reunite

Elias returns to his home, where Salome is waiting for him. Their reunion shows the love and affection they have for one another.

02

Salome asks him to stay

Salome wants Elias to leave his dangerous life behind so that they can live together peacefully. She tells him that Mindoro would be an ideal place to live peacefully.

03

Elias hesitates

Elias worries that his enemies could discover him and put Salome in danger.

04

Salome offers him a place

Salome offers Elias a place to stay, showing her willingness to care for him despite the risks.

05

Elias leaves

Elias chooses to continue his dangerous journey rather than settle down with Salome.

The Missing Pages

Why did Rizal remove the chapter?

The removal of the chapter was connected to the practical difficulties Rizal faced while preparing the novel for publication.

A difficult decision

Fewer pages. Lower printing cost.

Rizal had limited funds and was concerned that his money would not be enough to cover the printing costs of the novel.

Because of this, he removed the chapter and some lines from the manuscript to reduce the number of pages and save money.

  • “Elias and Salome” was removed before printing.
  • Rizal later felt sorry for eliminating this part of Elias's story.
  • According to the supplied material, Rizal viewed Elias as an ideal person to lead a revolution rather than Simoun.
  • Rizal also thought he might not have enough time to write a sequel.
End of Part II

The story was only the beginning.

Noli Me Tangere does not only tell the story of individual characters. Their experiences also reveal larger problems in society — ideas that lead us to the next part of the presentation: how these characters and themes can still be represented in the present society.
