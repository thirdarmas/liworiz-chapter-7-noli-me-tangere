Part III · Characters & Society

Meet the characters behind Rizal's social message.

Before looking at the bigger themes of Noli Me Tangere, let us first understand the people who bring those ideas to life. Each character has a different background, personality, struggle, and role in society.

Characters → Themes → Relevance Today
First: The Characters

Who are they?

The characters of Noli Me Tangere come from different social positions and experiences. By looking at their backgrounds and personalities, we can better understand the different problems Rizal presented in the novel.

01
The Reformist

Crisóstomo Ibarra

Juan Crisóstomo Ibarra y Magsalin

Ibarra is the wealthy young protagonist of the novel who returns to the Philippines after studying in Europe. He comes home hoping to continue his father's vision and improve his community.

Brief Background

  • Son of Don Rafael Ibarra.
  • Studied in Europe for several years.
  • Returned to the Philippines after completing his studies.
  • Planned to establish a school in San Diego.
  • Was engaged to María Clara.
  • Faced opposition from Padre Dámaso and Padre Salvi.
  • His experiences eventually changed his view of society.
Personality & Representation

Education, idealism, and peaceful reform

Ibarra is intelligent, educated, idealistic, ambitious, compassionate, determined, and reform-minded. At first, he believes that education and peaceful reform can improve society.

In today's society, he can represent students, professionals, teachers, young leaders, and other educated Filipinos who want to use their knowledge to create positive change.

His desire to build a school also shows the importance of education. At the same time, his experiences show that good intentions and education may not always be enough when a system is controlled by people with greater power.

02
The Beloved

María Clara

Ibarra's fiancée

María Clara is Ibarra's childhood sweetheart and fiancée. She was raised in a wealthy and influential family and is closely connected to the religious and social elite of San Diego.

Brief Background

  • Daughter of Kapitán Tiago and Doña Pia Alba.
  • Ibarra's childhood sweetheart and fiancée.
  • Raised in a wealthy and influential family.
  • Her relationship with Ibarra is affected by powerful figures.
  • Padre Salvi uses information about her mother to pressure her.
  • It is later revealed that Padre Dámaso is her biological father.
  • Her circumstances eventually lead her to the Santa Clara convent.
Personality & Representation

Social expectations and personal freedom

María Clara is loving, gentle, compassionate, respectful, religious, obedient, and emotional. However, she is often pressured by the expectations of her family and society.

She can represent people, especially women, whose personal decisions are strongly influenced by family expectations, traditional ideas, social status, and judgment from others.

Her story can also be connected to issues involving personal freedom, gender expectations, family pressure, and social judgment.

03
The Voice of Suffering

Elías

The fugitive boatman

Elias is a mysterious boatman who becomes one of Ibarra's closest and most trusted friends. Unlike Ibarra, he has a closer connection to people who experience suffering and inequality.

Brief Background

  • Comes from a family with a history of suffering and injustice.
  • Experiences abuse, accusations, and discrimination.
  • Helps Ibarra when he is being pursued.
  • Repeatedly risks his own safety to help others.
  • Sacrifices himself while helping Ibarra and Basilio.
Personality & Representation

Marginalized voices and social change

Elias is brave, selfless, compassionate, loyal, courageous, patriotic, and sacrificial. He is deeply concerned about the suffering of others.

In today's society, Elias can represent marginalized people and individuals who speak against injustice even when they have less power, wealth, or social influence.

He can also be connected to advocates, activists, and individuals who fight for deeper social change.

04
The Powerful Friar

Padre Dámaso

Dámaso Verdolagas

Padre Dámaso is a powerful Franciscan friar who has significant influence over the people of San Diego. He has a long history of conflict with Don Rafael Ibarra and strongly dislikes Crisóstomo.

Brief Background

  • A powerful Franciscan friar.
  • Has significant influence in San Diego.
  • Has a long history of conflict with Don Rafael Ibarra.
  • Repeatedly insults and humiliates Crisóstomo Ibarra.
  • Influences several important events in the story.
  • Later revealed to be María Clara's biological father.
Personality & Representation

Abuse of authority and privilege

Padre Dámaso is arrogant, proud, aggressive, abusive, judgmental, powerful, manipulative, and conscious of his social position.

He represents the abuse of authority and privilege. In today's society, he can be connected to powerful individuals who use their position to intimidate, control, or unfairly treat others.

His character raises questions about power, privilege, influence, and accountability.

05
Hidden Power & Manipulation

Padre Salvi

Bernardo Salvi

Padre Salvi is an influential friar in San Diego. Unlike the more openly aggressive Dámaso, Salvi appears quiet and reserved, but he secretly uses his influence to control situations around him.

Brief Background

  • Another influential friar in San Diego.
  • More quiet and reserved compared with Padre Dámaso.
  • Develops an interest in María Clara.
  • Uses information about her mother's past to pressure her.
  • Manipulates situations involving Ibarra.
  • His influence continues to affect María Clara's life.
Personality & Representation

Manipulation behind a respectable image

Padre Salvi is quiet, manipulative, strategic, jealous, secretive, hypocritical, patient, and controlling.

He represents hidden manipulation and abuse of influence. Unlike obvious forms of abuse, manipulation can happen quietly through secrets, threats, pressure, or personal information.

His character reminds us that abuse of authority is not always loud or aggressive. Sometimes, it can happen behind a respectable appearance.

06
The Influential Filipino

Kapitán Tiago

Don Santiago de los Santos

Kapitán Tiago is a wealthy and influential Filipino businessman whose social position is closely connected to the colonial authorities and religious elite.

Brief Background

  • Lived between Binondo in Manila and San Diego.
  • Did not receive formal education.
  • Worked under a Dominican priest.
  • Built connections with the Church and Spanish authorities.
  • Became a wealthy Filipino businessman.
  • Became gobernadorcillo, or town leader.
  • Married Doña Pia Alba, who died giving birth to María Clara.
  • Was close to Padre Dámaso and Padre Salvi.
Personality & Representation

Social status and conformity

Kapitán Tiago is submissive, obedient, status-conscious, selfish, and hypocritical. His religious devotion is also connected to his desire to maintain favor with powerful friars.

In today's society, he can represent wealthy or influential people who cooperate with powerful individuals or institutions to maintain their social position.

He also represents people who compromise their own values and principles in exchange for approval, protection, or status.

07
The Independent Thinker

Pilosopo Tasyo

Don Anastasio

Pilosopo Tasyo is an intelligent and educated Filipino whose ideas are often considered strange or crazy by the people around him.

Brief Background

  • Came from a wealthy family.
  • Studied in college but did not finish.
  • His mother wanted him to live according to her wishes.
  • Known for his deep thoughts and ideas.
  • Often considered "crazy" because of his unusual ideas.
  • Recognized problems caused by the abuse of power.
Personality & Representation

Critical thinking and speaking the truth

Pilosopo Tasyo is intelligent, patriotic, and independent-minded. He is not afraid to look at society differently from other people.

He can represent teachers, researchers, journalists, students, and other people who use knowledge to question society and expose injustice or corruption.

His character also reminds us that people with different perspectives are not necessarily wrong. Sometimes, new ideas are misunderstood simply because they are different.

08
The Mother

Sisa

A mother caught in suffering

Sisa is the mother of Crispín and Basilio. Her story shows the suffering of an ordinary mother whose family is affected by poverty, abuse, and injustice.

Brief Background

  • Mother of Crispín and Basilio.
  • Has a cruel and abusive husband.
  • Deeply loves and cares for her children.
  • Suffers greatly after Crispín disappears.
  • Becomes desperate to find her children.
  • Loses her sanity after overwhelming grief and suffering.
Personality & Representation

Vulnerable families and mothers

Sisa is loving, self-sacrificing, patient, vulnerable, and helpless in the face of the problems surrounding her family.

She can represent mothers who experience domestic violence, poverty, and social injustice while trying to protect their children.

Beyond mothers, Sisa can also represent vulnerable people who suffer greatly when society and its institutions fail to protect them.

9
Hope for the Future

Basilio

Sisa's older son

Basilio is the older son of Sisa and the older brother of Crispín. Like his brother, he experiences poverty, fear, and suffering caused by people and institutions with greater power.

Brief Background

  • Older son of Sisa.
  • Older brother of Crispín.
  • Works as a sacristan alongside Crispín.
  • Experiences poverty, fear, and suffering.
  • Is punished by people connected to the church.
  • Often tries to escape dangerous situations.
  • Discovers that his mother is missing.
Personality & Representation

Resilience and the hope of young people

Basilio's experiences show a young person forced to deal with hardship at an early age.

He can represent children and young people who experience poverty, family problems, and difficult circumstances but still have the possibility of building a better future.

His character reminds us why young people need protection, education, opportunities, and support instead of being trapped by the circumstances they were born into.

10
Colonial Mentality

Doña Victorina

Victorina de los Reyes de Espadaña

Doña Victorina is a woman obsessed with appearing Spanish. She looks down on other Filipinos and believes that Spanish identity makes her socially superior.

Brief Background

  • Obsessed with appearing Spanish.
  • Married to Don Tiburcio de Espadaña.
  • Her husband pretends to be a doctor.
  • Looks down on other Filipinos.
  • Considers herself superior because of her Spanish image.
Personality & Representation

Colonial mentality and identity

Doña Victorina is status-conscious, arrogant, pretentious, and insecure.

She represents people who reject their own identity and culture because they believe another culture is superior.

Her character is strongly connected to colonial mentality. She reminds us that a person's worth should not depend on how closely they imitate another culture, but on their own character, values, and actions.

From Characters → Central Themes

Different lives. One bigger picture.

From Ibarra's desire for reform to Elias's sacrifice, from Sisa's suffering to Doña Victorina's colonial mentality, each character reveals a different problem within society. When we put their stories together, we begin to see the larger message of Noli Me Tangere.

Second: Central Themes

What is Rizal trying to show?

The characters may have different stories, but their experiences connect to several larger ideas. These themes help us understand the social problems Rizal placed under the spotlight.

01

Injustice

The novel portrays the struggles and injustices experienced by Filipinos during Spanish colonial rule. The experiences of characters such as Elias, Sisa, and Crispín show how unfair systems can deeply affect ordinary people.

Fairness · Equal treatment · Human dignity
02

Abuse of Power

Noli Me Tangere shows how people in positions of authority can influence or control the lives of ordinary people. Characters such as Padre Dámaso and Padre Salvi demonstrate different forms of power and influence.

Power · Privilege · Accountability
03

Education

Ibarra's desire to establish a school reflects the importance of education in his vision for improvement. Education is presented not simply as personal achievement, but as something that can contribute to the development of society.

Knowledge · Opportunity · Development
04

Social Change

The characters respond to their society in different ways. Some seek peaceful reform, some question the existing system, while others suffer because they have little power. Together, their stories encourage readers to recognize social problems instead of simply accepting them.

Reform · Awareness · Action
05

Sacrifice

Sacrifice can be seen in characters who put the welfare of others before their own comfort and safety. Elias is one of the clearest examples, as he repeatedly risks himself to help others.

Service · Courage · Responsibility
06

Social & Family Pressure

María Clara's story shows how personal choices can be affected by family expectations, social status, religion, and the people around her. This theme remains understandable today because many people still experience pressure regarding relationships, education, careers, identity, and personal decisions.

Family · Expectations · Personal freedom
07

Love for the Country

Rizal dedicated Noli Me Tangere to the Philippines, his fatherland. The novel presents the suffering of Filipinos and encourages readers to recognize the problems affecting their society. Love for one's country is therefore connected not only to pride, but also to caring about its people and problems.

Patriotism · Responsibility · Service
From Themes → Present Society

1887 is not 2026. But some questions remain.

The Philippines today is very different from the society Rizal lived in. However, the problems explored in the novel give us a way to reflect on fairness, power, education, inequality, identity, and our responsibility toward others.

Third: Relevance Today

Why does Noli still matter?

We should not assume that the Philippines today is exactly the same as it was during Rizal's time. Instead, the novel gives us ideas that we can use to examine problems that may still appear in different forms.

Rizal's 19th Century Context

Filipinos experienced discrimination and social inequality under colonial rule.
Powerful individuals and institutions could influence the lives of ordinary people.
Education was presented as an important path toward personal and social improvement.
Characters experienced society differently depending on their social position and power.
Families and individuals could suffer when they had little power to challenge the system around them.

Our Present-Day Perspective

We can still discuss fairness, discrimination, equal treatment, and human dignity.
Questions about accountability and the responsible use of power remain relevant.
Education continues to be connected with opportunity and personal development.
People continue to experience different realities depending on their social and economic circumstances.
We can use the lessons of the novel to become more aware of injustice and the experiences of vulnerable people.
The bigger message

The characters are different.
The problems are connected.

Through the lives of Ibarra, María Clara, Elias, Padre Dámaso, Padre Salvi, Kapitán Tiago, Pilosopo Tasyo, Sisa, Crispín, Basilio, and Doña Victorina, Rizal presented different sides of Filipino society. Their stories help us understand the novel's larger concerns about injustice, power, education, social change, sacrifice, identity, and responsibility.

End of Part III

From the pages to the world.

We have now looked at the characters, the central themes, and their possible relevance to society today. From here, we can move to Rizal's journey through Europe after the publication of Noli Me Tangere and his eventual return to the Philippines.
