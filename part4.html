Part IV · Rizal's Europe Tour

The Europe Tour after Noli Me Tangere.

After the publication of Noli Me Tangere in Berlin, José Rizal and Maximo Viola traveled through several parts of Europe before Rizal eventually continued his journey toward the Philippines.

Follow the route
1887

The year of the journey

From Berlin, onward.

Following the publication of Noli Me Tangere, Rizal and Maximo Viola traveled through several European cities and countries. The journey eventually led Rizal toward Marseille, Saigon, and finally Manila.

Rather than presenting the trip as one simple line on a map, the journey can be viewed as a series of encounters with different places, cultures, political systems, and historical environments.

The Journey

A European itinerary

Each stop provides a different part of Rizal's journey following the publication of his first novel.

I
May 11, 1887

Dresden

Germany

On May 11, 1887, Rizal and Maximo Viola left Berlin and headed to Dresden, Germany.

They were warmly welcomed by Dr. Hans Meyer, the German anthropologist Rizal had met during his earlier visit to Leipzig.

Historical setting

Germany in 1887

During this period, Otto von Bismarck was Chancellor of the German Empire. Germany was also undergoing industrial, railway, and scientific development.

II
Following Dresden

Leitmeritz

Present-day Litoměřice, Czech Republic

Rizal and Viola continued to Leitmeritz, where Rizal met Ferdinand Blumentritt.

Blumentritt is one of Rizal's closest European correspondents and supporters.

A significant meeting

Rizal & Blumentritt

Their meeting marked an important point in Rizal's relationship with the Austrian scholar and his continuing intellectual exchange with European scholars.

III
Central Europe

Prague

Bohemia

Rizal and Viola proceeded to Prague and visited the tomb of Nicolaus Copernicus.

Copernicus is remembered for proposing the heliocentric model, in which the Earth and other planets revolve around the Sun.

Intellectual history

Copernicus

The visit connects Rizal's journey with the history of European scientific thought and astronomy.

IV
Continuing westward

Brno · Vienna · Salzburg · Munich · Nuremberg · Ulm

Central Europe

Rizal and Viola continued their journey through several cities in Central Europe, including Brno, Vienna, Salzburg, Munich, Nuremberg, and Ulm.

Vienna was the capital of the Austrian half of Austria-Hungary and was an important center of culture, music, and architecture.

Munich was a major cultural center in the Kingdom of Bavaria, within the German Empire.

Cities & culture

Ulm

In Ulm, they climbed the tallest and largest cathedral in Germany.

This cathedral is possibly the Ulm Minster.

V
First week of June 1887

Switzerland

Basel · Bern · Lausanne · Leman Lake · Geneva

Rizal and Viola entered Switzerland during the first week of June 1887. They visited Basel and Bern, passed through Lausanne and Leman Lake, and eventually reached Geneva.

Rizal was deeply saddened when he received news about Igorot people who had been exhibited at an exposition in Madrid, Spain. According to historical accounts, they faced ridicule and degrading treatment, and reportedly died. Their G-strings or clothing and weapons were used as objects of mockery by the people who viewed that exposition. Indigenous Filipinos were treated as curiosities instead of people deserving respect and dignity.

Compared with the monarchies they had visited, Switzerland offered a different example of how a European country could organize its government.

Political setting

The Swiss Confederation

Switzerland was a federal republic in which authority was divided between the federal government and its cantons.

It was also known for political independence and its multilingual population.

VI
After Switzerland

Italy

Venice · Florence · Rome

Maximo Viola eventually parted ways with Rizal and returned toward Spain while Rizal continued to Italy.

Rizal visited Venice, Florence, and Rome. He admired the architecture, streets, and historical remains of the country.

Historical setting

A recently unified Italy

Italy had become a unified kingdom during the nineteenth century, with Rome becoming its capital in 1871.

VII
July 3, 1887

Marseille

France

After visiting Italy, Rizal traveled to Marseille, France, arriving there on July 3, 1887.

He boarded the Djemnah, the same ship he had used when he traveled toward Europe five years earlier.

Port city

Marseille

Marseille was an important Mediterranean port and served as a major point for international sea travel.

VIII
July 30, 1887

Saigon

Present-day Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam

Rizal sailed from Marseille toward Saigon, which was under French colonial rule during this period.

On July 30, 1887, Rizal reached Saigon and boarded the Haiphong to continue his journey toward Manila.

Final stage

Toward home

From Saigon, Rizal's journey moved toward the Philippines after his European travels.

✦ His Return · August 1887 ✦
The Journey Home

Manila

August 5, 1887

On the quiet evening of August 5, 1887, the steamer Haiphong dropped anchor in Manila Bay. After five years of study and struggle across Europe, José Rizal stood once again upon his native soil, bringing with him the revolutionary impact of the newly published Noli Me Tangere.

End of Part IV

The journey ends.

His story continues.

Rizal's European journey after the publication of Noli Me Tangere eventually brought him back to the Philippines. His travels became another part of the larger story surrounding his life, ideas, and work.
